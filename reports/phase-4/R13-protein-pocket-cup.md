# R13: The "protein pocket" cup (cold brew plus a protein powder the drinker releases)

Desk research only, done on 2026-10-20. I used public web pages and contacted no one. Every number is tagged with a source [SRC-R13-NN], or is marked ESTIMATED (my own arithmetic or judgement) or UNKNOWN (I could not find it). Prices are in the currency the source used, and USD is converted to CAD at roughly 1.38, which is ESTIMATED.

---

## 0. Short answer

- **The idea already exists for bottles.** Push caps and twist caps that hold powder above a drink are a mature product (Karma, Vessl, Tap the Cap, and many factory-made generic caps). **No off-the-shelf disposable *cup* with a sealed powder pocket turned up.** Disposable lids with a pocket exist only as patents.
- **The bigger problem is size.** Most cap chambers hold about **1–10 g** of powder [SRC-R13-03][SRC-R13-04][SRC-R13-05]. A real plant-protein serving is a **~46 g packet** for 21 g of protein [SRC-R13-14]. A standard dispensing cap therefore can't hold a full serving.
- **The "hidden bottom" cup is real**, and the closest real-world model is 7-Eleven Philippines' "City Cafe Blind Cups" from August 2025. You tear a strip, the bottom half separates, and a charm is inside [SRC-R13-07][SRC-R13-08]. The same shell could carry a **sealed retail protein sachet** in place of a toy, with no co-packer filling the cup.
- **Mixing is solvable but not instant.** Cold liquid plus plant protein clumps when stirred. It needs a **lid and a 10–20 second shake** (ESTIMATED time) [SRC-R13-10][SRC-R13-11][SRC-R13-12].
- **The cheapest legal test is low-tech.** Use a plain cup and lid, a **sealed, Canada-labelled retail single-serve protein packet** held on with a band or sticker, and a "tear, pour, lid, shake" ritual. A custom dual-chamber cup needs 20,000–70,000-unit minimums [SRC-R13-05][SRC-R13-16] and is not worth it before demand is proven.

## 3. Practical issues

### 3a. Will pea or plant protein mix into cold brew in seconds?

| Issue | What the sources say | What it means for you |
|---|---|---|
| Cold liquid | "Protein powders mix poorly in very cold water" [SRC-R13-10]. Protein powder repels cold coffee and forms blobs [SRC-R13-11]. | Iced cold brew is the hardest case. |
| Stirring | Stirring with a spoon doesn't disperse powder well; a shaker or frother does [SRC-R13-10]. Shaking works for cold coffee [SRC-R13-11]. | A straw stir will leave clumps. **Plan for lid on + shake.** |
| Order of adding | Powder poured in first and liquid poured on top compacts and leaves dry pockets. Liquid first, then powder, is better [SRC-R13-11]. | A **bottom** compartment is the wrong way round: powder underneath liquid clumps. Powder from a **lid or sachet poured on top**, then shaken, is better. |
| Pea protein specifically | Standard pea protein has struggled with grittiness and sediment in drinks. Newer "high solubility" pea isolates are marketed as grit-free (vendor claim) [SRC-R13-13]. | Pick a powder sold as "instant" or "mixes easily" and test it in your own cold brew before an event. |
| "Instantized" powders | Agglomeration and a lecithin coating make powder wet and sink faster [SRC-R13-12]. | Ask your supplier whether the powder is agglomerated or lecithinated. |
| Shake time | No source gives seconds for cold brew. | ESTIMATED 10–20 s of hard shaking in a sealed cup. **Test it.** |

**Lid needed:** a normal sip-hole or straw-slot lid will spray when shaken. Use a flat lid with the straw hole covered by a sticker until after shaking, or a cup and lid rated for shaking. Leak-tightness is ESTIMATED and UNKNOWN for any given cup and lid pair, so test it.

**Headspace:** a 46 g packet [SRC-R13-14] is bulky. Fill coffee to about 10–12 oz in a 16 oz cup so there is room to shake (ESTIMATED).

### 3b. Keeping powder dry next to a cold drink

- Dry powders pick up water readily. Small amounts of water vapour cause clumping, texture loss, and possible microbial growth. Foil or high-barrier laminates keep out moisture, oxygen and light [SRC-R13-19].
- Commercial caps use a **foil seal** that is pierced on activation [SRC-R13-05].
- Most protein powders have a shelf life of about **1–2 years** when kept dry [SRC-R13-10].
- **Implication:** sealed foil stick packs or sachets are already a proper barrier. A home-made paper pocket or plastic compartment next to an ice-cold, sweating cup is not. Condensation is the enemy, so keep the sachet on the outside of the cup or in a dry outer shell, not under ice.

### 3c. Who is allowed to fill a two-chamber cup?

- **Toronto Public Health rules for special events** [SRC-R13-15]:
  - All food sold or given away must come from an **approved source**, meaning a facility inspected by a regulator. You must keep purchase records.
  - Food made at home can't be sold unless that home business has been inspected.
  - At the event, preparation is limited to **"final assembly and cooking"**. Chopping, mixing and other processing must be done beforehand in an inspected premises.
  - Applications are due **at least 3 weeks** before the event.
  - A certified food handler must be on site.
  - Any booth that portions or serves needs a proper **handwashing station**. Booths selling only pre-packaged, low-risk foods may use 70–90% alcohol sanitizer instead.
- **What this means (my reading, not legal advice):**
  - Pouring loose protein from a tub into your own little bags or cup pockets is **repackaging/processing**. It would need to be done in an inspected kitchen (commissary or co-packer), not by hand at the booth or at home.
  - Handing out an **unopened retail packet** with a cup involves no repackaging.
  - Scooping protein from a commercial tub straight into a customer's drink at the booth is arguably "final assembly", like adding syrup. **Confirm with TPH when you file the vendor form.** Status: UNKNOWN until confirmed.

### 3d. Labelling a sachet (Canada)

| Situation | Requirement | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Any consumer pre-packaged food (your own white-label sachet) | Mandatory info in **English and French** | [SRC-R13-17] |
| Nutrition Facts table | Required on pre-packaged food | [SRC-R13-18] |
| Exemptions that could apply | Individual servings sold for immediate consumption; items sold only at fairs or markets **by the individual who prepared them** (not foods made by another company); packages under 15 cm² | [SRC-R13-18] |
| **Exemption is lost** if you make a nutrient claim, e.g. "20 g protein", or add vitamins or minerals | A protein claim removes the exemption, so the table is needed | [SRC-R13-18] |
| Test-market relief | A CFIA Test Market Authorization can waive **bilingual** labelling for up to **12 months**. Apply about **6 weeks** ahead on form CFIA/ACIA 5868. Common name and net quantity still required. | [SRC-R13-17b] |

**Simplest route:** use a protein packet **already sold at retail in Canada**, which should already carry bilingual labelling and Nutrition Facts. Keep it sealed. Note that US-market packets (e.g. Orgain US) are probably **not** bilingual (ESTIMATED), so buy Canadian-market stock. Some protein powders in Canada are licensed as Natural Health Products (NPN) rather than foods, and how that interacts with a drink you sell is UNKNOWN. Flag it to R11 and R5-R7.

### 3e. Cup material in Toronto

Items labelled compostable or biodegradable, **including coffee cups**, are **not accepted in Toronto's Green Bin** and must go in the garbage [SRC-R13-24]. A "compostable" PLA cup costs more but is landfilled in Toronto, so don't make a green claim around it.

- A Canadian stick-pack co-packer (Crosby, Canada) fills 2–25 g sticks, but pilot runs **start at 70,000 units** [SRC-R13-16]. That is far too many for a 2–3 event test.

### Recommended test sequence

1. **Event 1 (Option A).** Plain cup and flat lid. A sealed Canadian retail plant-protein packet held to the cup with a printed paper band ("DROP THE BASS: tear, pour, lid, shake"). Have a sticker over the straw hole for shaking. Track: % of people who actually add the powder, shake complaints, clumps.
2. **Event 2 (A vs B, side by side).** Offer "add protein yourself" (A) vs "we shake it in" (B) to see whether the self-release ritual is worth roughly $1.50–2.50 more per cup (ESTIMATED).
3. **Event 3 (only if the ritual wins).** Order a small run of 7-Eleven-style tear-off bottom shells with the sealed packet hidden inside as the "drop". Only after real demand, talk to cap or cup suppliers about a true sealed chamber, knowing a full protein dose won't fit standard 3–10 g caps.
