# R4 + R6: Format comparison and manufacturer longlist (coffee-protein, white label)

Prepared for: Ashish (GTA, Ontario). Budget: CAD 10,000–15,000 total. Method: desk research only, public web pages, accessed 2026-10-18. Nobody was contacted, no forms filled, no quotes requested, no accounts created.
Currency: supplier prices are USD as published. Not converted (no FX source checked).

---

## Plain-language summary

1. A **powder** (coffee + protein in a pouch) is the only format where the published minimums fit a CAD 10–15k budget with room left for labels, testing, freight and marketing.
2. **Ready-to-drink (RTD) dairy protein coffee** is a low-acid drink. In Canada it must be commercially sterile (retort or aseptic) or kept refrigerated (SRC-R4-05). Those lines usually need tens of thousands of units per run (SRC-R4-06), which is over budget.
3. **RTD plant protein** has the same sterility rules. The one small-run RTD coffee canner with a published price (2,400 cans) lists non-dairy add-ins only and no protein line (SRC-R6-13).
4. The best-fit suppliers on paper are Canadian powder blenders: Free From Co-Packing (NS), Gourmet Nutrition (Canada) and Canadian Premier Supplements (Windsor ON). A US stock "protein coffee" pouch at 60 units (SRC-R6-14) is a cheap way to taste-test. None of the MOQs are confirmed.
5. Regulatory classification (food vs supplemented food vs natural health product) depends on how the product is labelled and marketed (SRC-R4-03, SRC-R4-04). Treat it as a **question for counsel** before you print labels.

## Recommendation (R4) — a recommendation, not a decision

**Recommendation:** start with a **powder** (c or d). Run a two-step test.
1. **Taste and market test, roughly USD 1.6–3.2k:** a stock protein-coffee pouch from a published low-MOQ source (60–144 units, SRC-R6-14). Use it for tastings and feedback from friends and gym contacts only, until Canadian labelling is confirmed.
2. **First Canadian-labelled run:** a Canadian dry blender (Free From Co-Packing, Gourmet Nutrition or Canadian Premier Supplements), once MOQ and price are confirmed. Whey or plant should be decided by the taste test.

**Why:** a powder needs no retort or aseptic process. It avoids the low-acid canned-food sterility rule (SRC-R4-05). It ships about 10× lighter per serving (ESTIMATED), and it has the only published minimums that fit CAD 10–15k. RTD dairy protein coffee is the most attractive retail format, but aseptic and retort minimums are far over budget (SRC-R4-06). Revisit RTD after the powder proves demand.

## Shortlist (R6)

1. **Free From Co-Packing (NS)**: the only Canadian supplier found that names both coffee blends and protein blends, with pilot batches and pouches. The facility is dairy-free, so it fits option (d) plant only.
2. **Gourmet Nutrition F.B. (Canada)**: stock formulas plus custom, low MOQ, NPN/GMP support. That helps if counsel says the powder is a natural health product.
3. **Canadian Premier Supplements (Windsor ON)**: makes both whey and vegan protein powders, Health Canada site licensed, "low MOQ". It is closest to the GTA, which helps with pickup and freight. Fits options (c) and (d).
4. **Smash Pharma via Pietra**: the only published price and low MOQ (60 units) for an actual protein-coffee product. Best for a cheap taste test. US labels, not for Canadian retail as-is.
5. **Nutrient Survival (NV)**: published 500 lb start-up pilot and SQF. A backup if Canadian MOQs turn out high. Cross-border freight and duties UNKNOWN.

## Questions to ask suppliers later (only once contact is approved)

1. Exact MOQ per SKU and per flavour, in units and kg, for a pilot run? Is a stock base available, with my label only?
2. Price per unit at MOQ, 2× and 5× MOQ. What do set-up, plate, formulation and sample fees cost?
3. Do you have, or can you blend, a coffee + whey/milk or coffee + plant protein formula? What protein and caffeine per serving?
4. Which certifications are current (SQF/BRCGS level, organic, GMP, Health Canada site licence)? Do you hold a **Safe Food for Canadians licence**? Can you share the certificate copies?
5. Who is responsible for Canadian labelling (bilingual, Nutrition Facts, supplemented-food caution identifier if applicable)? Do you review labels?
6. Is the product made as a food or as an NHP (NPN)? Who holds the licence?
7. Stated shelf life and the stability data behind it. Packaging format (pouch, tub, stick packs) and oxygen barrier?
8. Lead time from PO to ship? Payment terms (deposit %)?
9. Allergen controls: is dairy, soy or gluten on site?
10. Freight: pallet or parcel to GTA, typical cost, and whether you can ship direct to customers.
11. (RTD suppliers) Retort or aseptic? Minimum run? Can it run dairy protein at ~15–20 g per serving?

- The last ~27k characters of the Health Canada supplemented-foods guidance were not read (caffeine supplemental-ingredient entries may be there).

**Guessed (ESTIMATED)**
- Shelf lives (RTD 9–12 months; powder 12–24 months).
- Freight weights (can ≈0.39 kg; pouch ≈0.9–1.2 kg) and the ~10–15× weight ratio.
- RTD dairy first-run capital "USD 20k+" (assumes ~USD 1–2/unit at tens of thousands of units; the per-unit figure is not sourced).
- 500 lb pilot ≈ 250 thirty-serving pouches (arithmetic at 30 g/serving).
- Actus location (Wisconsin), inferred from the "WI Dept of Agriculture" certification.
- That US-labelled stock products need relabelling for Canada (logic; only NutriCraft states its white label is US-only).

**Unsure**
- Whether a protein coffee with **no** added vitamins, minerals, amino acids or caffeine is a "supplemented food" or a plain food, and whether a powder would be food or NHP. **Question for counsel.**
- Whether Ashish (as brand owner using a contract manufacturer) needs his own SFC licence. CFIA ties licences to activities such as labelling for interprovincial trade and importing (SRC-R4-07). **Question for counsel.**
- Whether Nations Brewing's pasteurized nitro coffee cans meet low-acid commercial-sterility rules. Not stated on the page.
- "Low MOQ" claims (Canadian Premier, Gourmet) have no numbers.
- Supliful shipping to Canada and Canadian label compliance: UNKNOWN.
