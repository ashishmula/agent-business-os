# R3: Positioning routes for a coffee-protein product (Canada first, US second)

Prepared: 2026-10-18 · Desk research only (public web pages; nobody contacted, no forms, accounts, purchases or downloads run) · Owner: Ashish (GTA) · Budget CAD 10,000–15,000 · No existing audience · DEC-0003: Canada first (GTA in person + online Canada), US second.

> Not legal advice. Every figure has a source ID or is marked ESTIMATED, UNKNOWN or ANECDOTAL. IDs from earlier drafts (SRC-R0-.., SRC-R1-.., SRC-R4-.., SRC-R6-..) point to the source tables in those files. Organizer attendance numbers are as reported in local media, not audited.

---

## 1. Plain-language summary (5 lines)

1. **A stock ("white label") recipe with your logo on it is not a reason to buy.** Brüst, Javvy, Chike and the Starbucks and Tim Hortons lattes already exist. The difference has to come from **who it is for and where you meet them**, and later from your own recipe.
2. **Best route: a daytime "coffee rave" brand** (techno and house without alcohol: coffee parties, sober morning raves, run clubs and fitness classes with DJs, DJs and producers), **sold as a dairy-free, bilingual Canadian powder.** Toronto has a real scene: one coffee-party series grew from about 250 to 1,400 people in five events [SRC-R3-04].
3. **Second route: Canada's plant-based protein coffee** (pea or oat, English and French label). The only plant RTD found in Canada was out of stock [SRC-R1-15], and 42% of Canadians bought a plant-based dairy product in a year [SRC-R3-22].
4. **Keep away from bars, clubs and alcohol.** Health Canada warns against mixing caffeine with alcohol, and adding caffeine to alcoholic drinks is illegal [SRC-R3-15]. Do not use the words "muscle building", "energy" or "recovery" on the pack until counsel has approved them.
5. **Spend in two steps.** First, a cheap taste and channel test (about CAD 3–5k, ESTIMATED). Then put the rest into one Canadian-labelled run, and only if fixed kill numbers are passed. If both lead routes fail their kill tests, stop: that is the no-go.

## 2. White label vs private label: what the supplier pages actually show

| | **White label (stock recipe, your label)** | **Private label (custom recipe, yours alone)** |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | A supplier's existing formula, non-exclusive. Others can sell the identical product. You change only the label and pouch [SRC-R3-19] | A formula made for you: ingredients, dose and flavour. Exclusive [SRC-R3-19] |
| Published MOQ / cost | **Smash Pharma via Pietra:** stock pea-protein coffee, 30 servings, printed pouch, **60 units at USD 25.99, 96 at 23.69, 144 at 21.99** [SRC-R3-20] (= USD 1,559 at 60). **NutriCraft:** 1,000 units, "CAD 8–12/unit", CAD 8–12k start, 4–6 weeks; its white label is **US-only** [SRC-R3-19]. **Supliful:** "no MOQ" dropship, US [SRC-R6-15] | **NutriCraft:** 1,000 units at "CAD 12–18/unit" plus CAD 5–10k R&D, so **CAD 15–25k total, 12–16 weeks** [SRC-R3-19]. **Nutrient Survival (NV):** 500 lb start-up pilot, price UNKNOWN [SRC-R6-09]. **Canadian blenders** (Free From Co-Packing, Gourmet Nutrition, Canadian Premier Supplements): "low MOQ" or pilot batches, **no figures** [SRC-R6-01, R6-05, R3-21] |
| Canada fit | US stock labels do not meet Canadian bilingual and Nutrition Facts rules [SRC-R0-06, R0-07]. **Taste tests only, not retail.** | Needed for any product sold in Canada, unless a Canadian blender offers a stock base with a Canadian label (UNKNOWN; ask later) |
| Caveat | NutriCraft prices are labelled "typical ranges for demonstration purposes" and assume a supplement (NPN) route, not a food [SRC-R3-19]. Treat them as an order-of-magnitude guide only | Same caveat. A food-route Canadian dry blender may cost less (UNKNOWN) |

**Working model (ESTIMATED):** a "semi-custom" run, meaning a Canadian blender's base protein-coffee blend with one flavour change and a bilingual label, is the most likely fit for the budget. No public price exists, so this must be confirmed with suppliers once contact is approved.

## 3. Routes

Competitor reference points used in every route: **Brüst**: Canadian RTD with 20–30 g dairy protein, 50–175 mg caffeine, CAD 2.99–3.99 per bottle; it targets "people who love coffee… on the go", including young professionals and "the elderly" [SRC-R1-04, R1-07, R3-28]. **Javvy**: US DTC powder with 10 g whey, USD 39.95 for 20 servings [SRC-R2-07, R2-08]. **Starbucks protein lattes**: in Canada since Sept 2025, 15–36 g [SRC-R0-20]; US price projected at USD 6.25–8.25 (estimate by a third-party site, not menu prices) [SRC-R3-27]. **Tim Hortons protein lattes**: 17–20 g [SRC-R0-19]; price not checked.

### Route A: "Daytime rave fuel", a techno/coffee-rave brand (Ashish's idea, redirected)

- **Exact buyer:** GTA adults aged 22–40 (ESTIMATED) who go to **alcohol-free daytime** dance events: coffee parties, sober morning raves, run clubs and fitness classes with DJs. Also DJs and producers who drink coffee through long studio sessions or daytime sets. **Not** late-night club crowds.
- **One-line promise:** "Real coffee and plant protein for dancing in daylight: no alcohol, no crash-and-burn night."
- **Why over Brüst, Javvy or the café lattes:** Brüst and Javvy sell gym-and-commute convenience. Starbucks and Tim Hortons sell a café menu item. Nobody found owns the sober-dance identity, the merch-style design or a presence at the events themselves. Add a dairy-free bilingual recipe (Route B spec) and there is also a product reason. **Honest caveat:** this difference is brand and channel, which others can copy. The Coffee Party already has its own coffee brand, Houseblend Coffee [SRC-R3-01], and Nabob sponsored its own CNE coffee party with DJs [SRC-R3-11].
- **White label or custom:** White label (Smash Pharma stock, 60 units, USD 1,559 [SRC-R3-20]) is enough **for the channel test only**. Selling in Canada needs a Canadian-labelled semi-custom run (MOQ UNKNOWN [SRC-R6-01, R6-05]). If you want a signature flavour ("after-hours mocha", for example), that is private label: NutriCraft's guide figure is CAD 15–25k [SRC-R3-19], over budget, so delay it.
- **Format fit:** **Powder** (stick packs or pouch) for sale. It fits the budget, ships light and suits DJs' bags [SRC-R4 matrix]. Hand out samples at events as mixed cold cups. Branded RTD cans would suit events better, but MOQs are tens of thousands of units [SRC-R4-06]: later.
- **Evidence of demand (scene is real; protein demand inside it is UNKNOWN):**
  - **The Coffee Party (Toronto):** monthly Saturday daytime party, alcohol-free at launch, CAD 10 including drinks and pastries [SRC-R3-02]. Attendance went from 250 (Nov 2024) to 500, then 1,000 with "another 1,000" reportedly at the door [SRC-R3-01], then about 1,100 [SRC-R3-03], then 1,400. At its fifth event (outdoor, The Well, Apr 2025) it served 4,000+ coffees and 3,000+ pastries, but got complaints about lines and coffee quality [SRC-R3-04]. In June 2025 it held a festival at Stackt Market, nearly sold out, with resale at CAD 30 [SRC-R3-05]. Vol. 12 (Jul 2025) sold out [SRC-R3-06]. Later espresso martinis were sold extra [SRC-R3-03], so not every event is strictly dry. Status in 2026: **not verified**.
  - **House Brew Dance Party (Toronto):** "Where Coffee Meets the Beat", Vol. 2 on 24 Jan 2026 at IDK Social Bar & Cafe, with unlimited coffee and local house DJs [SRC-R3-07].
  - **Daybreaker Toronto:** sober morning dance at the CN Tower, Aug 2025, CAD 115, with free matcha and "coffee shots" [SRC-R3-08]. Daybreaker cites 800,000 participants worldwide [SRC-R3-08]. A Toronto chapter is still active: "Heatwave", 11 Dec 2026, CAD 84 [SRC-R3-09].
  - **Ravers Run Club:** weekly Sunday 5 km on the waterfront. **Morning Disco Toronto:** monthly, with caffeinated drinks [SRC-R3-10] (2026 status not verified).
  - **MotionHaus #1:** workout + live DJs + dance party, 16 Oct 2026, St Clair W [SRC-R3-12].
  - Historical context: Daybreaker first came to Toronto by 2016 at CAD 20–30 [SRC-R3-13]. "Coffee raves" also exist abroad (e.g., Barcelona, free entry, 139 "interested" on RA) [SRC-R3-14].
  - Post-festival: VELD runs 3 days at Downsview, tickets from CAD 254.18 [SRC-R3-17]. Attendance not published there (UNKNOWN).
  - DJ/producer population in Toronto or Canada: **UNKNOWN** (no figure found).
- **First 100 customers from zero (ESTIMATED):**
  1. Weeks 1–3: brand name, logo and merch-style pouch design. 60 stock pouches for a private taste panel only (not for sale) [SRC-R3-20]. Cash about CAD 2,100 for product (USD 1,559 at an assumed FX of 1.37, not checked) + CAD 800–1,500 design. 25 hours.
  2. Weeks 3–8: approach 6–8 daytime organizers and DJ-led fitness classes (House Brew, MotionHaus, run clubs, Daybreaker). Offer free iced protein-coffee shots plus a QR pre-order code. Toronto special-event vendors must apply about 21 days ahead, use an approved food source and have a certified food handler at the booth [SRC-R3-29]. Cash CAD 0–500 per event for booth or sponsorship (UNKNOWN), plus CAD 100–150 per event for cups and ice. 8–10 hours per event.
  3. In parallel: send 10 Toronto DJs or producers a free pack for honest content (no paid claims). CAD 300–600 in product. 15 hours.
  4. Convert: pre-orders on a simple online shop (fees not checked) count toward the 100 only once the Canadian-labelled run ships.
  - **Total before inventory:** about CAD 4,000–7,000 cash and 120–160 hours (ESTIMATED). Inventory for the Canadian run is on top (MOQ and price UNKNOWN).
- **Canadian regulatory and claim risks:**
  - **Alcohol:** no sampling at alcohol-serving events, no co-branding with bars, no "pre-game" or cocktail recipes. Health Canada warns caffeine can mask intoxication, and caffeine added directly to alcoholic drinks is illegal [SRC-R3-15]. LCBO caps natural caffeine in alcoholic drinks at 30 mg per serving [SRC-R3-16]. Note: some coffee parties sell espresso martinis [SRC-R3-03]. Ask organizers to keep your stand away from the bar, or skip those events.
  - **Claims:** "recovery", "energy", "stay up all night" or "muscle" wording may push a powder toward a natural health product, or count as a health claim [SRC-R0-10]. Say "post-festival brunch", not "post-festival recovery".
  - **Rave and drug association:** a brand image near stimulant use is a reputational risk (ANECDOTAL/ESTIMATED; no source checked). Keep imagery daytime, sober and community.
  - **Minors:** Daybreaker lets children in [SRC-R3-08]. Don't hand caffeinated samples to children (ESTIMATED good practice).
  - Plus the base rules: bilingual label, Nutrition Facts, front-of-pack symbol, SFC licence question [SRC-R0-06, R0-07, R0-11, R0-08].
- **What changes for the US:** the scene is bigger and older there (Daybreaker started in NYC [SRC-R3-13]; The Coffee Party did a Miami pop-up [SRC-R3-01]). But you can't reach events in person from the GTA, de minimis no longer applies to Canadian parcels [SRC-R0-15], and FDA has warned about caffeinated alcoholic drinks [SRC-R3-16]. Use the US for online DJ-community sales only after Canada works.
- **Kill criteria:**
  1. Fewer than **10% of people who taste a sample scan the QR code**, or fewer than **3% pre-order**, across 600+ samples at 4 or more events.
  2. Fewer than **25% of first-order buyers reorder within 45 days** (online, first 60 buyers).
  3. Fewer than **3 of 8 organizers** agree to free sampling, which means the channel is closed.

### Route B: Canada's plant-based, bilingual protein coffee

- **Exact buyer:** Canadian adults who avoid dairy (vegan, flexitarian, lactose-sensitive), shopping online and at GTA natural-food stores and markets.
- **One-line promise:** "Dairy-free protein coffee, made and labelled in Canada, in English and French."
- **Why over Brüst, Javvy or the cafés:** Brüst, Two Bears, Premier, Javvy and Chike are all dairy-based [SRC-R1 table]. OWYN plant RTD was out of stock at Walmart.ca, and Vega's plant powder was not verified in Canada [SRC-R1-15, R1-23]. Starbucks and Tim Hortons lattes are dairy-protein café drinks (dairy-free option not checked).
- **White label or custom:** A stock pea-protein coffee exists (Smash Pharma) for the taste test [SRC-R3-20]. To sell in Canada, use a Canadian dairy-free blender: Free From Co-Packing names coffee and protein blends, pouches and pilot batches in a top-allergen-free plant [SRC-R6-05]. MOQ and price UNKNOWN. Plant off-notes are a known risk [SRC-R4-06], so flavour work may be needed (custom).
- **Format fit:** **Powder.** Plant RTD has the same sterility and MOQ problem as dairy [SRC-R4-05, R4-06].
- **Evidence of demand:** 42.2% of Canadians consumed a plant-based dairy product in the past 12 months, and half of those weekly. But 49.2% prefer animal protein and only 22.3% find plant-based affordable (n = 5,507, 2023) [SRC-R3-22]. Lactose malabsorption is cited at about 44% of Canadians, and self-reported intolerance at 16% (figures conflict on the page) [SRC-R3-23].
- **First 100 customers (ESTIMATED):** vegan and natural-food markets (2–4 stalls at CAD 100–300 each, UNKNOWN), Reddit and vegan Facebook-group posts made as yourself (no spam), and 20 micro-creators with free packs. CAD 3,000–5,000 before inventory; 100–140 hours.
- **Regulatory and claim risks:** a "source of protein" claim needs a protein rating of ≥8 (PDCAAS) [SRC-R0-05]. Pea protein must meet it (check the supplier's data). "Dairy-free" or "vegan" wording plus allergen cross-contact: a question for counsel. No "muscle" claims on powder [SRC-R0-10].
- **What changes for the US:** the US plant segment is already served (OWYN at HEB, Vega) [SRC-R1-14, R1-23]. The bilingual angle doesn't matter there. Weaker in the US.
- **Kill criteria:**
  1. Blind taste test: fewer than **60% of 40 tasters** rate it 4/5 or higher against a dairy competitor.
  2. Fewer than **20% repeat** within 45 days among the first 60 buyers.
  3. Landed cost above **CAD 2.00 per serving** at your MOQ (ESTIMATED ceiling to sell at about CAD 2.70–3.60, in line with the Canadian powder band [SRC-R1 price bands]).

### Route C: Decaf / half-caf protein coffee ("the after-2 pm protein coffee")

- **Exact buyer:** afternoon and evening coffee drinkers, caffeine-sensitive adults, and people cutting back on caffeine for sleep.
- **One-line promise:** "All the coffee taste and protein, without the 3 a.m. ceiling stare."
- **Why over competitors:** every product found had 50–225 mg caffeine; no decaf protein coffee was found [SRC-R1 table]. Brüst's lowest is 50 mg [SRC-R1-06].
- **White label or custom:** **custom needed.** No stock decaf protein-coffee base was found. Private-label guide figure is CAD 15–25k [SRC-R3-19], over budget unless a Canadian blender quotes lower (UNKNOWN). A Canadian decaf supplier (Swiss Water) is cited in the trade press [SRC-R3-25]; its supply terms were not checked.
- **Format fit:** powder.
- **Evidence of demand:** global, not Canadian. Decaf product launches rose 30% from 2022 to 2023 (Mintel, via trade press). Half of 18–40-year-old frequent decaf drinkers cite better sleep (Swiss Water survey, US) [SRC-R3-25]. Nabob served a decaf at its Toronto coffee party (ANECDOTAL) [SRC-R3-11]. Canadian decaf share: UNKNOWN [SRC-R3-26 has no decaf figure].
- **First 100 (ESTIMATED):** online-led (sleep and wellness creators, office wellness). Harder from zero. CAD 4,000–6,000; 140+ hours.
- **Risks:** lowest caffeine risk. Avoid "improves sleep" (health claim). The decaf label term may need counsel.
- **US:** larger decaf market; same custom-recipe cost.
- **Kill criteria:** (1) fewer than **30% of 200 surveyed coffee-protein buyers** say they would switch to decaf or half-caf; (2) a quoted MOQ cost above **CAD 8,000**; (3) fewer than 20% repeat among the first 50 buyers.

### Route D: Older adults ("protein in the coffee you already drink")

- **Exact buyer:** Canadians aged 55+ who drink coffee daily, and adult children who buy for parents.
- **One-line promise:** "Your morning coffee, with the protein most of us don't get enough of." (Wording must be cleared by counsel.)
- **Why over competitors:** softer flavours, hot-mixable powder, large-print bilingual label (ESTIMATED). **But Brüst already names "the elderly" as a target** [SRC-R3-28], so this gap is weaker than first thought.
- **White label or custom:** custom likely needed (hot-water solubility, lower sweetness; ESTIMATED).
- **Format fit:** powder (hot drink).
- **Evidence:** Canadian guidance is 0.8 g/kg/day. A Quebec study found older adults at about 1.0 g/kg/day, and 1.2 g/kg/day was linked to 40% less muscle loss over 3 years (secondary, 2018) [SRC-R3-24]. Share of Canadians aged 65+: not retrieved (UNKNOWN).
- **First 100 (ESTIMATED):** community-centre and pharmacy demos, seniors' fairs. Slow and trust-heavy. CAD 4,000–6,000; 160+ hours.
- **Risks:** highest claim risk. Muscle, ageing or "sarcopenia" wording is a health claim and points toward an NHP [SRC-R0-10]. Do not use it.
- **US:** a large market but a claims minefield (FDA/FTC; not checked).
- **Kill criteria:** (1) fewer than **15 of 50** older tasters say they would buy at CAD 2.50+ per serving; (2) counsel says no usable claim wording exists; (3) fewer than 20% repeat.

### Route E: Real-coffee-taste-first (specialty roaster collaboration)

- **Exact buyer:** coffee lovers who think protein coffee tastes like chalky sports drinks.
- **One-line promise:** "A protein coffee a barista would actually drink."
- **Why over competitors:** the gap is a named Toronto roaster's beans and a taste-first story. **But Chike ("real coffee") and Javvy already claim taste** [SRC-R1-13, R2-07]. Coffee-party crowds also complained about coffee quality [SRC-R3-04], which is ANECDOTAL support.
- **White label or custom:** **custom** (specific roast). CAD 15–25k guide [SRC-R3-19]. Over budget.
- **Format fit:** powder (instant/freeze-dried coffee needed; sourcing UNKNOWN).
- **Evidence:** 71% of Canadians drank coffee yesterday [SRC-R3-26]. 21% have cut out-of-home coffee spending and 15% tried a new home brand [SRC-R3-26]. That points toward at-home options, but not specifically protein.
- **First 100 (ESTIMATED):** café counters and markets. CAD 5,000–8,000; 120 hours.
- **Risks:** low regulatory risk. A roaster partnership needs contact (later).
- **US:** very crowded.
- **Kill criteria:** (1) loses a blind taste test to Chike or Javvy with **under 50%** preference (n ≥ 40); (2) roaster costs push landed cost above **CAD 2.20 per serving**; (3) fewer than 20% repeat.

## 4. Scoring

Weights: **Differentiation vs Brüst/Javvy/café lattes 25%**, **Evidence of demand 20%**, **Launch cost fits CAD 10–15k (white label or semi-custom possible) 20%**, **Reachable from zero audience in GTA 20%**, **Regulatory/claim risk 15%** (5 = lowest risk). Scale 1–5. Scores are judgement (ESTIMATED) based on the evidence above.

| Route | Diff (25%) | Demand (20%) | Cost fit (20%) | Reach (20%) | Reg risk (15%) | **Weighted** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Daytime rave fuel** | 4 | 3 | 4 | 4 | 3 | **3.65** |
| **B. Plant-based bilingual** | 4 | 3 | 4 | 3 | 4 | **3.60** |
| C. Decaf / half-caf | 5 | 2 | 2 | 2 | 5 | **3.20** |
| E. Real-coffee-taste-first | 3 | 3 | 2 | 3 | 4 | **2.95** |
| D. Older adults | 3 | 3 | 3 | 2 | 2 | **2.65** |

Maths (A): 0.25×4 + 0.20×3 + 0.20×4 + 0.20×4 + 0.15×3 = 3.65. The other rows use the same formula.

**Pass rule (set before scoring):** weighted ≥ 3.50, no criterion scored 1, and a first test possible for ≤ CAD 5,000. **A and B pass. C, D and E do not**, mainly on cost (they need a custom recipe) or reach.

**Recommendation:** **combine A and B.** Use the **daytime-rave brand and event channel (A)** to sell a **dairy-free, bilingual Canadian powder (B)**. A gives a way to reach the first 100 people with no audience. B gives a product reason that Brüst and Javvy can't match today. The A vs B gap (3.65 vs 3.60) is within scoring noise, so the stage-1 tests decide: if event conversion fails but blind taste and online repeat pass, drop the rave branding and run plain Route B. Keep decaf (C) as a later SKU.

**Honest "none pass, no-go" check:** A and B pass only on paper. Neither has direct evidence that people **buy protein coffee** at these events, or that plant protein coffee **repeats**. If, after about CAD 5,000 and 8 weeks, **both** routes miss their kill criteria (conversion under 3%, repeat under 20%, or failed taste test), then **no route passes. Stop before the Canadian inventory run** and keep the remaining CAD 5–10k. A score of 3.65 out of 5 is not strong. The biggest unknown is the Canadian co-packer MOQ and price. If the semi-custom run costs more than about CAD 8,000 (ESTIMATED), the budget no longer covers inventory plus marketing, and that is also a no-go.
