# R1–R2: Coffee-protein competitors and unit pricing (Canada first, US second)

Desk research only. All pages accessed 2026-10-18. Every figure carries a source ID; ESTIMATED / UNKNOWN / ANECDOTAL are marked. No page I read contained instructions addressed to AI agents (see Flags for spam and lookalike sites).

## Plain-language summary

1. In Canada, the main home-grown player is **Brüst**, a Canadian protein cold brew (20–30 g dairy protein, 330 mL) sold at Well.ca, Walmart.ca, Amazon.ca, Goodness Me and Costco Business Centre. Premier Protein Café Latte is the big imported rival (SRC-R1-01, -04, -05, -07).
2. Smaller Canadian or Canadian-sold options include Two Bears Protein Latte, Trema Café (Quebec), Raw Nutrition RTD, Smarter Protein Coffee powder and Chike powder. Most US RTDs (Slate, Iconic, Super Coffee, Starbucks Coffee & Protein) did not turn up at the Canadian retailers I checked (SRC-R1-08..13).
3. Almost every product uses **dairy protein** and has **50–225 mg caffeine**. I found no decaf or low-caffeine product and only one plant-based RTD (OWYN, out of stock at Walmart.ca) (SRC-R1-14, -15).
4. In Canada, an RTD single costs about **CAD 3.79–5.69**, and Brüst multipacks work out to **CAD 3.00–3.75 per bottle**. That is about **CAD 1.50–2.20 per 10 g protein**, versus about **USD 1.49–1.81** for US RTDs (Price table).
5. Openings to test (hypotheses): a plant-based or lactose-free RTD that is Canadian-made with a bilingual label; a decaf or half-caf high-protein option; and a 30 g / low-sugar product at about CAD 3.50 per unit.

## Gaps (hypotheses: to validate in later steps)

- **H1 Plant-based RTD in Canada.** The only plant RTD found (OWYN) was out of stock at Walmart.ca. Vega's plant powder is not verified in Canada. All Canadian-brand RTDs are dairy (SRC-R1-15, -04, -08). Test: demand for an oat/pea protein cold brew.
- **H2 Decaf / low-caffeine high-protein.** Every product found has 50–225 mg caffeine. I saw no decaf. Possible audiences: afternoon/evening drinkers, older adults, pregnancy-cautious buyers. Unsure whether demand is real.
- **H3 Bilingual Canadian-compliant label.** The cheaper US RTDs (Slate, Iconic, Super Coffee, Starbucks bottled) were not found at the Canadian retailers checked, possibly because of label or regulatory barriers. Health Canada's supplemented-food rules apply to added caffeine and some added ingredients (SRC-R1-29). Unsure: whether protein-added coffee counts as a supplemented food. Needs a regulatory check.
- **H4 Canadian price premium.** Canadian RTD costs about CAD 1.50–2.20 per 10 g protein, against about USD 1.49–1.81 in the US (Price table). A Canadian product at about CAD 3.00–3.50 per 20–30 g unit could undercut Premier via iHerb, but it would compete directly with Brüst at Walmart.ca (CAD 2.99/unit).
- **H5 Older-adult / "healthy ageing" positioning.** No brand targets this group (ESTIMATED).
- **H6 Brüst is the incumbent "Canadian protein coffee".** It already holds the Canadian-made, lactose-free, grass-fed positioning. A new entrant would need a different angle (plant, decaf, specific flavour, 30 g at low sugar, local GTA café/gym channel).
