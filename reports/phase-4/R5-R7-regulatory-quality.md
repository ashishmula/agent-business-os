## 1. Plain-language summary (5 lines)

1. **Keep the recipe to coffee + protein + flavour (no added caffeine, vitamins, minerals or amino acids) and sell it as a drink mix.** It then most likely stays a **regular food**, not a supplemented food or an NHP. Health Canada treats powders "intended to be reconstituted for consumption as a beverage" as leaning toward food [SRC-R5-13]. Counsel must confirm this.
2. **You probably do not need your own SFC licence** if a **licensed Canadian co-packer** makes, packs and labels the product and you only sell it. CFIA says a business whose "sole activity is to interprovincially trade food" needs no licence, but the maker and labeller do [SRC-R5-02]. If you import, re-pack or re-label yourself, you need one (CAD 250 for 2 years [SRC-R5-23]).
3. **At Toronto events, everything "sold or given" (free samples too) must come from an approved source.** You need a certified food handler at the booth, a real handwashing station if you mix drinks on site, and vendor forms in at least 3 weeks ahead. The organizer notifies Toronto Public Health 30 days ahead [SRC-R5-07, R5-08]. No City fee was found; costs come from organizers and insurance.
4. **Words matter:** "muscle", "recovery", "performance" and dosing language point toward an NHP. "Energy" is murky. Stay with plain food words, never pair the brand with alcohol, and treat "source of protein" as a regulated claim [SRC-R5-06, R5-13, R5-20].
5. **Quality before scale:** pick a GFSI-certified (SQF/BRCGS/FSSC) co-packer, demand a CoA for every lot, test the first lot yourself (about CAD 800–1,100 for a full panel, ESTIMATED [SRC-R7-04]), run a 40–80-person blind taste test, and keep a written complaint and recall plan. CFIA says to notify it **immediately** of a food-safety issue [SRC-R7-01].

## 2. R5: Canada path for this route (with US notes)

### 2.1 Regular food vs supplemented food (SF) vs natural health product (NHP)

| Question | Finding | Status | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| What makes it an SF? | A prepackaged food in a permitted category with an **added** supplemental ingredient (vitamins, minerals, amino acids, **caffeine**, etc.). Protein shakes/dry mixes and instant coffee mixes are permitted categories. Protein alone is not described as making a food an SF | Carried over | SRC-R5-16, R5-30 |
| Does coffee's own caffeine trigger the SFR? | The 150 ppm trigger is written for beverages "with **added** caffeine". Coffee-only caffeine looks outside it | Carried over; **Question for counsel** | SRC-R5-16 |
| Powder: food or NHP? | Format alone does not decide: "both granulated and powder formats are consistent with classification both as foods and as NHPs". Powders "intended to be reconstituted for consumption as a beverage" lean toward **food**. Words like "resistance training", "muscle gains", "muscle building", "mass building" point to **NHP**. So do supplement-style retailing to sub-populations, measuring devices or dosing, and health claims "outside the context of a diet" | Confirmed (primary) | SRC-R5-13 |
| "Energy" wording | The same guidance says beverage mixes using "energy" or "energy drink" in their representation are typically treated as **foods**. But caffeinated energy drinks are regulated as SFs, and an "energy" message on a caffeinated product may draw SF or health-claim scrutiny (ESTIMATED) | Primary for the classification line; risk is **Question for counsel** | SRC-R5-13, R5-16 |
| Tax side effect | CRA's dietary-supplement sheet lists **protein powders** as not basic groceries (taxable), using factors like "supplement" wording, performance/physique claims, an emphasised nutrient such as "whey protein isolate", dosage, or powder form. A coffee drink mix might be treated differently | Confirmed (primary), but the sheet dates from 2004; **Question for accountant** | SRC-R5-10 |

**Working position (ESTIMATED, for counsel to confirm):** common name along the lines of "Protein coffee drink mix" / "Mélange pour boisson au café et aux protéines". Mix instructions should give a normal drink volume (e.g., 250–350 mL). Use a **scoop-free** pouch or single-serve stick packs, with no "take X daily" dosing. Use no sports-supplement imagery.

### 2.4 Protein claims and risky words

- **Any protein representation, express or implied** (including "made with whey protein" or "protein coffee" as a name) must meet the "source of protein" conditions. A reasonable daily intake needs a protein rating of **≥20 (FO-1/PER method) or ≥8 (PDCAAS)**. PDCAAS values from mixed sources can be combined; PERs cannot. The protein grams in the NFt are exempt from the claim rules [SRC-R5-06]. *Confirmed (primary).*
- Example (ESTIMATED): 20 g whey (PDCAAS about 1.0) gives a rating of about 20, which passes the PDCAAS test easily. 20 g pea (about 0.8) gives about 16, also a pass. The co-packer or supplier must supply PDCAAS data. The reasonable daily intake for a coffee drink mix in Schedule K was **not read** (UNKNOWN).
- Thresholds for "high" or "excellent source of protein" were **not read** today (UNKNOWN; check the Table of Permitted Nutrient Content Statements).
- "High quality protein" and "source of amino acids" are not permitted (carried over, R0).
- **Word risk table (ESTIMATED, Question for counsel):**

| Word / idea | Risk | Suggested alternative |
|---|---|---|
| "muscle", "gains", "build", "performance" | High: NHP indicator [SRC-R5-13] | "20 g protein per cup" (as an NFt-backed fact, once cleared) |
| "recovery", "post-rave recovery" | High: health/sports claim, NHP drift | "after-party brunch", "sunrise cup" |
| "energy", "boost", "stay up all night" | Medium: food-classified per HC [SRC-R5-13], but implies a caffeine effect; SF scrutiny; rave/drug association | "real coffee", "made for daylight" |
| "fuel" | Medium–low: lifestyle word, but with "performance" it reads as sports nutrition | Use only as brand voice ("dance-floor fuel"), never with physiological promises |
| "clean", "healthy" | Medium: implied health claim | Ingredient facts instead |
| Cocktail / "pre-game" / mixer | High: caffeine + alcohol | Never |

### 2.5 Caffeine labelling

- If caffeine comes **only from coffee**, the SFR caffeine statements ("contains caffeine", "high caffeine content", the cautionary statements) are tied to **added** caffeine [SRC-R5-16]. HC's 2010 guidance says there is no mandatory quantitative caffeine labelling and that the voluntary guidance does not apply to well-known sources like coffee [SRC-R5-19]. *Carried over; the 2010 document is old, so counsel should confirm it.*
- **Recommendation (ESTIMATED good practice):** voluntarily print "about X mg caffeine per serving / environ X mg de caféine par portion", **after lab verification** (section 3.2), and add "Not recommended for children or pregnant or breastfeeding women" if counsel agrees. Whether a voluntary caffeine statement on a non-SF is allowed and how it must be placed: **Question for counsel**.
- **Never add caffeine powder or guarana.** Doing so moves the product into the SFR (caution identifier, Supplemented Food Facts table) [SRC-R5-16, R5-30].

### 2.7 Caffeine and alcohol

Health Canada advises Canadians not to mix caffeinated drinks with alcohol, and adding caffeine to alcoholic drinks is illegal [SRC-R5-20, carried over, archived 2018 page]. LCBO caps natural caffeine in alcoholic drinks at 30 mg/serving (via 2011 CMAJ report) [SRC-R5-29]. **Rules for this brand (ESTIMATED policy):** no sampling at the bar or in licensed areas, no espresso-martini or cocktail content, no co-branding with alcohol brands or bars, and no "pre-game" language. Some coffee parties sell espresso martinis (R3), so ask organizers to place you away from the bar or skip those events. Whether AGCO rules restrict non-alcohol brand promotions at licensed events: **not checked / Question for counsel**.

### 2.9 Ontario sole proprietorship and HST

- **Business name registration (Ontario Business Registry):** **CAD 60** online, immediate. Renewal CAD 60 [SRC-R5-09]. *Confirmed (primary).* Validity period not stated on the page (5 years per general knowledge, ESTIMATED). Using only your own legal name needs no registration (general knowledge, ESTIMATED).
- **HST small supplier:** you are a small supplier if taxable supplies are **≤CAD 30,000 in any single calendar quarter and in the last four consecutive quarters**. Exceed it in one quarter and you are registered from the supply that crossed it, and **must register within 29 days**. Voluntary registration lets you claim input tax credits (e.g., the 13% HST paid on imports or on the production run) but locks you in for at least 1 year [SRC-R5-11]. *Confirmed (primary).* Ontario's 13% rate is listed among the HST rates but not tied to Ontario on that page (ESTIMATED 13%).
- R8 base case (~CAD 12,400 revenue in 6 months) stays under the threshold. **Decision (Question for accountant):** voluntary registration could recover about 13% of the production run (about CAD 880 on CAD 6,775, ESTIMATED), but you would then have to charge HST if the powder is taxable (protein powders are listed as taxable [SRC-R5-10]).

### 2.11 US notes (step 2)

- FDA does not pre-approve foods. The co-packer's facility needs **FDA food facility registration**, each shipment needs **prior notice**, and a US **FSVP importer** must be named [SRC-R5-25]. *Carried over.*
- Decide between **conventional food** and **dietary supplement** for the powder. FDA distinguishes beverages from supplements by representation, much as Canada distinguishes food from NHP [SRC-R5-27]. A drink-mix framing (as for Canada) is the consistent choice. **Question for US counsel.**
- Caffeine labelling is voluntary. FDA cites 400 mg/day for most adults [SRC-R5-26].
- **De minimis is suspended** for Canadian shipments (since 29 Aug 2025), so every parcel needs formal entry [SRC-R5-28]. Use a US 3PL with bulk entry (ESTIMATED).
- **Not checked:** California Prop 65 warnings. Protein powders, especially plant ones, are often tested for lead and cadmium. This is a reason to run the heavy-metals test (section 3.2) even for Canada.

### 3.3 Shelf life

- Durable life **>90 days** means a best-before date is **not mandatory**, but if one is shown it must use the bilingual format. The business determines durable life [SRC-R5-15]. *Confirmed (primary).*
- **Plan (ESTIMATED):** print a best-before date anyway (customers expect one; it also helps recalls). Start at **12 months**, even if co-packer data supports 18–24 months, until your own data exists. The limiting factors are coffee aroma staling, whey browning (Maillard), clumping from moisture pick-up, and plant off-flavours, not usually microbes.
- **Checks:** keep 6 retained pouches from lot 1 at room temperature (and 2 in a warm spot, about 30–35 °C, as an informal stress test). At months 0, 3, 6, 9 and 12, measure moisture and water activity (target aw <0.6, ESTIMATED), test mixability, and run a small sensory check (5 tasters, same scale as 3.4). Run micro at month 0 and at the end. Cost about CAD 200–600 over the year (ESTIMATED).
- **Packaging:** a high-barrier, resealable pouch with an oxygen and moisture barrier. Ask the co-packer for its validated shelf life for that film (QUOTE NEEDED).

| Where | **Round 1 (private, weeks 3–4):** friends, gym and coworkers, using stock pouches (not a public sale). **Round 2 (events, after counsel clears the samples):** at 2+ events, using the same recipe as the planned run |
| Products | **A** = our blend. **B** = a powder competitor bought at retail (e.g., Chike or Javvy), mixed the same way. Optional **C** = Brüst RTD poured into the same cup (reference only, flagged as RTD vs powder) |
| Blinding | Same white 2 oz cups, 3-digit random codes, same volume (30–40 mL), same temperature (iced, ≤4 °C for whey), prepared out of sight. Order **balanced** (half get A first, half B first) |
| Tasters | Adults only (19+ for clarity at mixed events, ESTIMATED policy); no pregnant people or people who avoid caffeine; allergen card (milk/soy) shown **before** tasting; water between samples |
| Sample size | **Minimum 40 per round; target 80 total** (40 private + 40 event). At n=40, a top-2-box result has about ±15 points of margin (95%); at n=80, about ±11 (R8 maths) |
| Scoring (paper or QR form, ≤60 seconds) | (1) Overall liking 1–5 per sample. (2) "Which do you prefer?" A/B (forced choice). (3) Just-about-right 3-point scales for sweetness, coffee strength and texture/chalkiness. (4) Purchase intent 1–5 at CAD 44 for 15 servings. (5) One free-text word |
| **Pass mark** | **All three:** (a) **≥60% of tasters rate A 4 or 5**; (b) **A preferred by ≥50%** over B (in the n=80 pooled result); (c) no just-about-right attribute with **>30% "too much" or "too little"** on the same side (ESTIMATED rule) |
| Borderline | 55–65% top-2 box, or 45–50% preference → reformulate the flavour once and retest with 40 more (R8 rule) |
| Fail | <55% top-2 box, or <45% preference → kill or rework before the production run |
| Records | Keep code sheets, sample lot numbers and raw scores (also the start of your complaint/traceability habit) |
| Event rules | Sampling at public events follows section 2.6: approved source, food handler, handwashing station, cold holding |

### 3.5 Complaint and recall handling with CFIA

1. **Complaint log (from day 1):** date, customer contact, lot code, best-before date, where bought, complaint type (taste, foreign matter, illness, allergic reaction, packaging), photos, and retained-sample check. Answer within 48 hours (ESTIMATED policy).
2. **Triage:** illness, an allergic reaction, foreign matter, or an undeclared allergen counts as a **food-safety complaint**. Hold all stock of that lot at once, and notify the co-packer the same day.
3. **Notify CFIA:** CFIA's recall guide says to **"immediately notify"** the CFIA area recall coordinator if CFIA is not already aware. Include complaint and illness details [SRC-R7-01]. Contacts are on the CFIA notification page [SRC-R7-02] (seen in search, not opened).
4. **CFIA's 12-step recall procedure** (best practice to meet SFCR s. 84(1) and s. 85): recall team, notify CFIA, identify the affected food, hold and segregate, distribution list, recall notice, effectiveness checks, control and disposal, corrective and preventive action, post-recall review [SRC-R7-01]. *Confirmed (primary).*
5. **Distribution list = your Shopify/Square order export** by lot. Keep each pouch's lot code on every order (scan or note at packing). For event sales, log the lot sold per event.
6. **Mock recall:** once before launch, then once a year (ESTIMATED frequency): pick a lot, and within 4 hours list every customer who got it and every unsold pouch.
7. **Records:** keep complaint, lot and distribution records for at least **2 years** (ESTIMATED, the SFCR traceability period; the period was not shown on the page read).
8. **Roles (ESTIMATED):** Ashish = recall lead and customer contact; co-packer = ingredient and production records. Agree this in the supply contract.
